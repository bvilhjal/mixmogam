\section{Overview and notation}

mixmogam fits the standard linear mixed model for genome-wide
association mapping,
\begin{equation}
  \y = \X\beta + u + e, \qquad
  u \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_g^2 \K), \quad
  e \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_e^2 I_n),
  \label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
where $n$ is the number of samples, $\X \in \R^{n\times q}$ the
covariate design (an intercept is included by default), and
$\K \in \R^{n\times n}$ a kinship (relationship) matrix. We write
$\delta = \sigma_e^2/\sigma_g^2$ and
$\V = \sigma_g^2(\K + \delta I_n)$. The covariance of \eqref{eq:model}
is parameterized by the single ratio $\delta$; all variance-component
estimation reduces to one-dimensional optimization of the REML or ML
profile likelihood over $\delta$.

The package implements four regimes:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Exact} ($n \lesssim 5{,}000$ by default): one dense
        eigendecomposition per leave-one-chromosome-out (LOCO) kinship;
        the association scan evaluates the exact EMMAX statistic in
        batched dense GEMMs (Sections~\ref{sec:fit}, \ref{sec:scan},
        \ref{sec:twostep}).
  \item \textbf{Two-step K-free} (large $n$): BOLT-LMM and LDAK-KVIK
        statistics with LOCO residuals from conjugate gradients or
        variational Bayes, never forming an $n\times n$ matrix
        (Section~\ref{sec:twostep}), with an optional structure-aware
        denominator (Section~\ref{sec:spectral}).
  \item \textbf{SLQ fitting} (large $n$): variance components by
        deflated stochastic Lanczos quadrature, requiring only
        matrix--vector products with $\K$ and no $O(n^3)$
        factorization (Section~\ref{sec:slq}).
  \item \textbf{Truncated spectrum}: a randomized top-$k$ eigenbasis of
        $\K$ with a flat-bulk tail (Section~\ref{sec:topk}). Kept for
        comparison only: its scans were anti-conservative in
        simulation, and they warn.
\end{enumerate}
Genotypes are stored sample-major as $n \times m$ matrices of
\texttt{int8} dosages in $\{0,1,2\}$ with $-1$ encoding missing calls;
SNP blocks are converted to \texttt{float32} with fused mean
imputation immediately before use, so the float working set is bounded
by the block size regardless of $m$.

\paragraph{Models and fits.} \texttt{LMM.fit()} returns an
\texttt{LMFit} that holds its model, so \texttt{fit.scan()},
\texttt{blup()} and \texttt{predict()} work on the fit alone. The model
caches the fit without that back reference and binds it on access
(\texttt{LMM.fit\_result}). When the two referenced each other, the cycle
kept $\K$ and its eigendecomposition alive until Python's cyclic garbage
collector ran: an exact LOCO scan stranded its 25 group models, about
8\,GB at $n = 4{,}000$.

\section{Variance-component estimation: exact EMMA}
\label{sec:fit}

\subsection{Restricted eigenspace}

Following Kang et al.\ (2008), the REML likelihood can be expressed
through the spectral decomposition of the \emph{restricted} matrix
$\Mm = \Sm(\K + I_n)\Sm$ with $\Sm = I_n - \X(\X^\top\X)^{-1}\X^\top$
(the v1 construction; eigenpairs beyond the $q$ annihilated directions,
offset by one). mixmogam evaluates the identical likelihood from a
\emph{single} eigendecomposition of $\K$ via the matrix determinant
lemma: with $V = \K + \delta I_n = \U \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i +
\delta) \U^\top$ and $W = \U^\top \X$, $w = \U^\top \y$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{RSS}(\delta) = w^\top D w - b^\top A^{-1} b, \quad
  A = W^\top D W,\;\; b = W^\top D w,\;\; D = \operatorname{diag}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\lambda_i + \delta}\bigr),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \ell_R(\delta) = \tfrac12\Big[p\big(\log\tfrac{p}{2\pi} - 1 - \log \mathrm{RSS}\big)
  - \big(\textstyle\sum_i \log(\lambda_i{+}\delta) + \log|A| - \log|\X^\top\X|\big)\Big],
  \label{eq:reml}
\end{equation}
with the derivative in closed form through $A' = -W^\top D^2 W$,
$b' = -W^\top D^2 w$. The two formulations agree to $5 \times 10^{-13}$
on likelihoods in the test suite; the lemma form replaces the second
$O(n^3)$ eigendecomposition with $O(nq^2)$ work.

\subsection{Profile likelihood and grid}

The REML profile log-likelihood is \eqref{eq:reml}. The ML variant
replaces $p$ by $n$ and drops the $\log|A| - \log|\X^\top\X|$ terms (same
quadratic form). Its equivalence with the restricted-eigenspace form is
asserted in the tests; v1's deliberate factor-of-two in the derivative function --
harmless for root finding -- is normalized. The grid
$\delta_j = \exp(\mathrm{linspace}(l_{\min}, l_{\max}, G{+}1))$ with
$G = 100$ and $[l_{\min},l_{\max}] = [-10, 10]$ is evaluated
\emph{vectorized over all grid points simultaneously} by broadcasting
$\lambda^R$ against $\delta$, so the whole grid costs $O(pG)$ fused
operations. The maximizing bracket is located by a sign change of
$\mathrm{d}\ell/\mathrm{d}\delta$ and polished with Brent's method
(v1 used Newton from the bracket midpoint; the root is identical and
Brent cannot diverge), with v1's boundary-acceptance and
grid-argmax fallback rules retained. Finally
\begin{equation}
  \hat\sigma_g^2 = \frac{s_1(\hat\delta)}{p}, \qquad
  \hat\sigma_e^2 = \hat\delta\,\hat\sigma_g^2, \qquad
  h^2_{\text{pseudo}} = \frac{1}{1+\hat\delta}.
\end{equation}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{EMMA exact fit (REML shown; ML analogous)}
\label{alg:emma}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $\y$, $\X$, $\K$, grid $(G, l_{\min}, l_{\max})$, tolerance $\tau$
\State $\Q \gets$ thin QR of $\X$;\quad $\Sm \gets I - \Q\Q^\top$
\State eigendecompose $\Mm = \Sm(\K + I)\Sm$; keep $\{\lambda^R_i, \eta_i\}_{i\le p}$
\State vectorize $\ell_R(\delta_j)$, $\ell'_R(\delta_j)$ over the grid $j=0..G$
\State $i^\ast \gets \arg\max_j \ell_R(\delta_j)$
\State find $i$ with $\ell'_R(\delta_{i}) > 0 > \ell'_R(\delta_{i+1})$ maximizing mean endpoint likelihood
\State $\hat\delta \gets \mathrm{Brent}(\ell'_R,\ \delta_i,\ \delta_{i+1},\ \tau)$; accept per boundary rules, else fall back to $\delta_{i^\ast}$
\State \Return $\hat\delta$, $\hat\sigma_g^2 = s_1(\hat\delta)/p$, $h^2_{\text{pseudo}}$, $\beta$ via \eqref{eq:gls}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{GLS coefficients and gBLUP}

With $\Hm \equiv \V^{-1/2}$ (applied implicitly, Section~\ref{sec:topk}),
\begin{equation}
  \hat\beta = \big(\X^\top\V^{-1}\X\big)^{-1}\X^\top\V^{-1}\y
  \quad\text{computed as}\quad
  \big((\Hm\X)^\top(\Hm\X)\big)^{-1}(\Hm\X)^\top(\Hm\y),
  \label{eq:gls}
\end{equation}
and the gBLUP of breeding values is
$\hat u = \hat\sigma_g^2\,\K\,\V^{-1}(\y - \X\hat\beta)$, with
$\V^{-1}$ applied through the eigenbasis.

\section{The association scan: EMMAX as batched GEMM}
\label{sec:scan}

\subsection{Single-SNP statistic}

For a candidate SNP $g \in \R^n$ (missing calls mean-imputed), v1
regressed the transformed, covariate-residualized phenotype on the
transformed, covariate-residualized SNP by an explicit least-squares
solve \emph{per SNP}. The identical statistic has a closed form.
Define, at the fitted $\hat\delta$,
\begin{equation}
  \Q \gets \operatorname{qr}\big(\Hm\X\big), \qquad
  r \gets (I - \Q\Q^\top)\,\Hm\y, \qquad
  \tilde g \gets (I - \Q\Q^\top)\,\Hm g, \qquad
  \mathrm{RSS}_0 \gets \|r\|^2 .
\end{equation}
The one-degree-of-freedom F statistic is the rank-one update
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{RSS}(g) = \mathrm{RSS}_0 - \frac{(\tilde g^\top r)^2}{\tilde g^\top\tilde g},
  \qquad
  F(g) = \Big(\frac{\mathrm{RSS}_0}{\mathrm{RSS}(g)} - 1\Big)(n - q - 1),
  \qquad
  p(g) = F_{1,\,n-q-1}\big(F(g)\big),
  \label{eq:scanstat}
\end{equation}
which equals v1's per-SNP regression exactly (Frobenius--Waugh--Lovell
partialling out). Effect sizes and standard errors come in closed form,
\begin{equation}
  \hat\beta_g = \frac{\tilde g^\top r}{\tilde g^\top \tilde g},
  \qquad
  \operatorname{se}(\hat\beta_g) =
  \sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{RSS}(g)}{(n - q - 1)\,\tilde g^\top\tilde g}} .
\end{equation}

\subsection{Batching}

All per-SNP quantities are rows of matrix expressions. For a block
$\G \in \R^{k\times n}$ of $k$ SNPs:
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde\G^\top = (I - \Q\Q^\top)\,\Hm\,\G^\top, \qquad
  \mathbf{num} = \widetilde\G\,^\top r \in \R^k, \qquad
  \mathbf{den} = \operatorname{diag}(\widetilde\G^\top\widetilde\G) \in \R^k,
\end{equation}
and \eqref{eq:scanstat} is applied elementwise. The transform
$\Hm\,\G^\top$ is two GEMMs against the eigenbasis (one GEMM per side);
the residualization is one small GEMM of cost $O(nqk)$. The scan is
therefore $O(n^2 m)$ dense linear algebra with BLAS-3 kernels, no
Python-level per-SNP loop, and no per-SNP factorization; arithmetic
defaults to \texttt{float32} (fitting stays \texttt{float64}).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Batched EMMAX scan}
\label{alg:scan}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require fitted $\hat\delta$; covariates $\X$; phenotype $\y$; genotype store returning blocks $\G \in \R^{k\times n}$; block size $k$, dtype
\State $\Q \gets \operatorname{qr}(\Hm\X)$; $r \gets (I-\Q\Q^\top)\Hm\y$; $\mathrm{RSS}_0 \gets \|r\|^2$; $\nu \gets n - q - 1$
\ForAll{blocks $\G$ from the store}
  \State $\widetilde\G^\top \gets (I - \Q\Q^\top)\,\Hm\,\G^\top$ \Comment{2 GEMMs vs.\ eigenbasis + 1 small GEMM}
  \State $\mathbf{num} \gets \widetilde\G^\top r$;\quad $\mathbf{den} \gets \operatorname{rowsums}(\widetilde\G \circ \widetilde\G)$
  \State $t^2 \gets \mathbf{num}^2 / \max(\mathbf{den}, \epsilon)$;\quad
         $\mathbf{RSS} \gets \max(\mathrm{RSS}_0 - t^2, 0)$
  \State $F \gets t^2 / \mathbf{RSS} \cdot \nu$;\quad $p \gets F_{1,\nu}(F)$ \Comment{batched survival function}
\EndFor
\State \Return $\{p, F, \mathrm{RSS}, \hat\beta_g, \operatorname{se}(\hat\beta_g)\}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Multi-df extensions}

2-df genotypic tests (v1's ANOVA path) build $L{-}1$ indicator
contrasts for an $L$-level SNP and solve the small normal equations.

\paragraph{GxE.} The 1-df interaction test is for $c_j = 0$ in
$\y = \X b + g_j a_j + (g_j \circ E) c_j + u + e$, with the SNP main
effect $a_j$ in the model and $E$ among the covariates (it is added when
absent): an interaction column tested without both main effects absorbs
them. With $\tilde g = (I-\Q\Q^\top)\Hm g$ and
$\tilde c = (I-\Q\Q^\top)\Hm (g\circ E)$, the interaction sum of
squares after the main effect is
$(\tilde c^\top r - \tfrac{\tilde c^\top\tilde g}{\tilde g^\top\tilde g}\tilde g^\top r)^2
 / (\tilde c^\top\tilde c - \tfrac{(\tilde c^\top\tilde g)^2}{\tilde g^\top\tilde g})$,
batched over the block; the 2-df joint test adds the main-effect
reduction. An optional polygenic interaction component with kinship
$\K\circ(EE^\top)$ (Sul et al.\ 2016) is fitted as a two-kinship mixture
(Section~\ref{sec:twokin}). The 2026-10-02 revision omitted $a_j$; with a
0/1 environment a pure main-effect SNP then gave interaction
$p \approx 10^{-6}$ in 20 of 20 replicates.

\paragraph{MLMM.} The stepwise multi-locus mixed model (Segura et al.\
2012) adds the most significant SNP as a fixed-effect cofactor,
re-estimates the variance components (REML for the scans) and repeats
until the pseudo-heritability has vanished or a step cap is reached,
then eliminates backward from the last forward model, dropping the
least significant cofactor (conditional F test) each step. Every
visited model is scored with the \emph{maximum-likelihood}
log-likelihood $\ell$ (REML likelihoods are not comparable across
fixed-effect designs) by the extended BIC (Chen \& Chen 2008),
$\mathrm{EBIC} = -2\ell + k'\log n + 2\log\binom{m}{k}$, and by mBonf, the
largest model whose cofactors all have conditional $p < \alpha/m$.
Plain BIC is reported but, as Segura et al.\ found, it is too tolerant
for GWAS.

\section{Large \texorpdfstring{$n$}{n}: the kinship as a streaming operator}
\label{sec:operator}

Following BOLT-LMM, above the dense regime the kinship is
never materialized. \texttt{GenotypeKinship} wraps the genotype store and
applies $K$ only through streaming products,
\begin{equation}
  K X \;=\; \frac{1}{m}\sum_{\text{blocks}} Z_b \bigl(Z_b^\top X\bigr),
\end{equation}
with $Z_b$ the Yang-2010 called-only standardized block; the
\texttt{scale\_k} normalization constants (mean off-diagonal, mean
diagonal) are obtained from two cheap streaming passes and folded into
the same products, so the operator equals the dense GRM to machine
precision (verified to $10^{-14}$ in the tests). Every large-$n$
computation is operator-native: the randomized eigensolver, the SLQ
solver of Section~\ref{sec:slq}, the scan transform (through the
truncated basis below) and gBLUP all consume mat-vec products only.
Cost per product is $O(nm)$ tall-skinny GEMMs with $O(\text{block})$
working memory, replacing $O(n^2)$ storage and an $O(n^2 m)$
materialization; the equivalence to the dense pipeline (variance
components, likelihood, gBLUP, scan under gentle truncation) is tested
directly.

\paragraph{Adaptive deflation.} The SLQ solver deflates only
\emph{well-separated} extreme eigenvalues (Ritz values at least
$4\times$ the bulk median): the analytic accounting for deflated
directions is exact only for an invariant subspace, and subspace
iteration cannot converge the boundary of a near-degenerate bulk.
Spectra without outliers deflate nothing -- the tight bulk is precisely
the regime Gauss quadrature resolves well.

\section{Large \texorpdfstring{$n$}{n}: truncated randomized spectrum}
\label{sec:topk}

This approximation is mixmogam's own (BOLT-LMM never truncates the
spectrum). A randomized top-$k$ approximation ($k = 1024$ by default)
is obtained from a Gaussian sketch $\Omega \in \R^{n\times(k+p)}$
($p = 12$ oversampling) powered by $\ell = 5$ subspace iterations,
$\Q_0 \gets \operatorname{orth}(\K\Omega)$, \
$\Q_t \gets \operatorname{orth}(\K\Q_{t-1})$, and the Ritz extraction
$B = \Q^\top\K\Q$, $B = W\Lambda W^\top$ yields
$\hat\Lambda_k, \hat U_k = \Q W_k$ (Halko et al.\ 2011). With the
discarded spectrum treated as a flat bulk at its mean
$\bar\lambda = (\tr\K - \sum_{i\le k}\hat\lambda_i)/(n-k)$,
\begin{equation}
  \Hm A \;=\; (\bar\lambda+\delta)^{-1/2} A \;+\;
  \hat U_k\Big[\operatorname{diag}\big((\hat\lambda_i + \delta)^{-1/2}\big) -
  (\bar\lambda+\delta)^{-1/2} I_k\Big]\hat U_k^\top A ,
  \label{eq:tail}
\end{equation}
exact when the discarded eigenvalues are equal. They are not: a GRM
bulk has a Marchenko--Pastur spread, and the per-SNP whitening error it
leaves inflates the scan. Re-read with the corrected $\lambda_{GC}$
(Section~\ref{sec:validation}), the 2026-10-03 large-$n$ simulation gave
$\lambda_{GC} = 2.13$, $1.28$ and $0.95$ at $k/n = 0.03, 0.13, 0.26$
($n = 4{,}000$; exact scan $0.91$) and $1.30$ at $k/n = 0.10$
($n = 10{,}000$; exact scan $0.85$; 84 vs 50 significant non-causal
SNPs). Large-$n$ association therefore goes
through the two-step statistics of Section~\ref{sec:twostep}.

\section{Large-\texorpdfstring{$n$}{n} fitting: deflated stochastic Lanczos quadrature}
\label{sec:slq}

Fitting $\delta$ without the full spectrum requires the spectral sums
$s_1, s_2, s_3, s_4$ of Section~\ref{sec:fit}. The SLQ solver
estimates them from Gauss quadrature rules built by Lanczos iteration
(Ubaru et al.\ 2017): with $T_t$ the tridiagonal Lanczos matrix after
$t$ steps from start vector $v$,
\begin{equation}
  v^\top f(\Am)\,v \;\approx\; \|v\|^2 \sum_{i=1}^{t} w_i f(\theta_i),
  \qquad (\theta, w) = \text{eigenpairs of } T_t,\ w_i = (S_{1i})^2,
\end{equation}
exact for polynomials of degree $\le 2t{-}1$ and spectrally accurate
for analytic $f$ such as $f_\delta(t) = (t + \delta)^{-1}$ and
$\log(t+\delta)$. Trace terms use unbiased Rademacher probe averages
($\E[z^\top f(\Am) z] = \tr f(\Am)$, probes pre-projected into the
covariate complement for REML); the quadratic forms in $\y$ use one
deterministic rule from the projected phenotype. All Lanczos runs use
full reorthogonalization ($t \le 96$ default, 12 probes), and they run as
one batched process: each step applies the operator to the $(n \times
13)$ block of all current Lanczos vectors (one GEMM pass over the
genotypes) while every run keeps its own basis, tridiagonal matrix and
breakdown test.

\paragraph{Deflation.} Gauss quadrature converges slowly when the
spectrum mixes extreme outliers (population structure) with a tight
bulk. Extreme eigenpairs of the operator are therefore estimated by a
randomized top-$d$ pass ($d = 128$) and handled analytically; the
quadrature resolves only the deflated bulk. Because the deflated
operator carries the removed directions as (near-)zero eigenvalues,
the probe estimates must be corrected by their contribution
$f_\delta(0)$:
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{i>p-d} f(\lambda_i) \;=\;
  \Big[\text{probe mean of } f_\delta\text{-rules}\Big] - d\,f_\delta(0),
  \qquad f_\delta(0) \in \{1/\delta,\ \log\delta,\ \delta^{-2}\}.
\end{equation}
Quadrature nodes are floored at zero to guard against roundoff-negative
deflated eigenvalues. On structure-rich kinships the resulting
$\hat\delta$ agrees with the exact EMMA fit to a few percent (the REML
profile is very flat near its optimum) and profile likelihoods agree to
about one nat; the total cost is $O(n^2)$ per matvec times
$O(\text{probes} \times \text{steps})$ matvecs, with no $O(n^3)$
factorization.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Deflated SLQ variance-component fit}
\label{alg:slq}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require operator $\mathrm{mv}(x) = \Sm\K\Sm x$; $\y$, $\X$; probes $P$, steps $t$, deflation $d$
\State $\hat\lambda, \hat U \gets$ randomized top-$d$ of $\mathrm{mv}$
\State $\mathrm{mv}_d(x) \gets \mathrm{mv}(x) - \hat U(\hat\lambda \circ (\hat U^\top x))$
\State $y_\perp \gets \Sm\y$; decompose $y_\perp = \hat U c + y_{\mathrm{rest}}$
\State rules: one Lanczos$(\mathrm{mv}_d, y_{\mathrm{rest}}, t)$; $P$ Lanczos$(\mathrm{mv}_d, \Sm z, t)$
\For{$\delta$ on grid and within Brent bracket}
  \State $s_1(\delta) \gets \sum_i c_i^2/(\hat\lambda_i{+}\delta) + \mathcal{Q}_y[f_\delta]$
  \State $s_2(\delta) \gets \sum_i \log(\hat\lambda_i{+}\delta) + \overline{\mathcal{Q}}_P[\log] - d\log\delta$ \Comment{deflation correction}
  \State $s_4(\delta) \gets \sum_i (\hat\lambda_i{+}\delta)^{-1} + \overline{\mathcal{Q}}_P[f_\delta] - d/\delta$
  \State assemble $\ell_R(\delta)$, $\ell'_R(\delta)$ as in \eqref{eq:reml}
\EndFor
\State \Return $\hat\delta$ by bracketing + Brent; $\hat\sigma_g^2 = s_1(\hat\delta)/p$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{LOCO association and two-step statistics}
\label{sec:twostep}

\paragraph{Why LOCO.} With the tested SNP inside the kinship, the
polygenic term absorbs part of its own effect (proximal contamination;
Listgarten et al.\ 2012, Yang et al.\ 2014): the exact EMMAX scan of the
2026-10-03 simulation was deflated ($\lambda_{GC} = 0.91$ at $n = 4{,}000$,
$0.85$ at $n = 10{,}000$). Every method behind \texttt{gwas()} therefore
tests SNPs on chromosome $g$ against a model built from the other
chromosomes. With more than 25 chromosomes (e.g.\ simulated data with one
``chromosome'' per LD block) consecutive chromosomes are merged into 25
groups of balanced size, BOLT-LMM's genome segments.

\paragraph{Exact LOCO.} For each group $g$,
$\K_{-g} = \operatorname{scale}\big((\sum_j z_jz_j^\top - \sum_{j\in g}z_jz_j^\top)/(m-m_g)\big)$
is formed by subtraction (two passes over the genotypes in total), the
variance components are refitted by exact EMMA, and the SNPs of group
$g$ are scanned with Algorithm~\ref{alg:scan}. Cost: one $O(n^3)$
eigendecomposition per group.

\paragraph{Strong environmental stratification.} Under
\eqref{eq:model} the variance of $\y$ along the $k$th eigenvector of
$\K$ is $\sigma_g^2\lambda_k + \sigma_e^2$, one function of the eigenvalue
for every direction. An environment that moves the phenotype along the
leading (ancestry) eigenvector by much more than that cannot be
represented. REML compensates by inflating $\sigma_g^2$, and SNPs that
load on the axis keep a residual association. Adding the top principal
components of $\K$ to $\X$ removes it (Price et al.\ 2006). In the sim
study's S2 (two demes at $F_{ST} = 0.02$; an environment on deme with 50\%
of the variance), exact LOCO gave $\lambda_{GC} = 1.27$, and null SNPs in
the top 1\% of loading on the environment had mean $\chi^2 = 2.27$. With
PC1 as a covariate these became 1.11 and 1.13 (archive
\texttt{20261003T122520Z-sim-study}). The non-LOCO scan's 0.98 is the
same residual offset by proximal-contamination deflation, so under
structure $\lambda_{GC}$ alone cannot rank LOCO against non-LOCO.

\paragraph{The streaming LOCO operator.} The two-step methods hold the
genotypes as standardized, covariate-projected blocks
$Z_b$ ($z \gets z - \Q\Q^\top z$, BOLT-LMM's treatment of covariates)
that never straddle a group. One pass applies \emph{every} LOCO kinship
to its own column of a block $P$: with per-group products
$S_h = Z_h^\top(Z_h P)$,
\begin{equation}
  \K_{-g}\,P_{:,r} \;=\; \frac{\big(\textstyle\sum_h S_h - S_g\big)_{:,r}}{M - M_g},
  \qquad g = \text{group}(r),
  \label{eq:locoop}
\end{equation}
so the $G$ LOCO systems of BOLT-LMM, the calibration solves, and the
LOCO eigenbases below all share GEMM passes.

\subsection{BOLT-LMM-inf}

Variance components $(\sigma_g^2, \delta)$ are estimated once on all
SNPs (Section~\ref{sec:slq}; BOLT-LMM uses Monte Carlo REML for the same
quantities) and are not refitted per group, BOLT-LMM's default. For
each group, $w_g = (\K_{-g} + \delta I)^{-1}\y$ is solved by batched
preconditioned conjugate gradients, the preconditioner inverting the
top-64 eigenpairs of $\K$ exactly and the rest as a flat bulk. Every
SNP $z_j$ in group $g$ then gets the retrospective statistic
\begin{equation}
  \chi^2_j = \frac{(z_j^\top w_g)^2}{c\,(z_j^\top z_j / n_{\mathrm{eff}})\,\|w_g\|^2},
  \qquad n_{\mathrm{eff}} = n - q,
  \label{eq:boltinf}
\end{equation}
whose calibration constant $c$ makes it match the exact prospective
statistic
$(z^\top \V_{-g}^{-1}\y)^2 / (z^\top \V_{-g}^{-1} z)$: for 30 random SNPs
with GRAMMAR $\chi^2 < 5$, $c_j = \sigma_g^2 z_j^\top(\K_{-g}+\delta I)^{-1}z_j
\,/\,\big((z_j^\top z_j/n_{\mathrm{eff}})\|w_g\|^2\big)$ by 30 more CG
solves, and $c = \overline{c_j}$ (Loh et al.\ 2015). The spread
$\mathrm{cv} = \operatorname{sd}(c_j)/c$ is reported: \eqref{eq:boltinf}
is exact only if the prospective denominator is proportional to
$z_j^\top z_j$ across SNPs, an assumption the BOLT-LMM authors flagged as
untested outside human data.

\subsection{BOLT-LMM}

The prior on standardized SNP effects is the two-Gaussian mixture
\begin{equation}
  \beta_j \sim p\,\mathcal{N}\!\Big(0, \tfrac{(1-f_2)\sigma_\beta^2}{p}\Big)
  + (1-p)\,\mathcal{N}\!\Big(0, \tfrac{f_2\,\sigma_\beta^2}{1-p}\Big),
  \qquad \sigma_\beta^2 = \sigma_g^2 / M,
\end{equation}
fitted by fully factorized variational Bayes (iterated conditional
posterior means). $(f_2, p)$ is chosen on BOLT-LMM's 18-point grid
$f_2 \in \{0.5, 0.3, 0.1\}$, $p \in \{0.5, 0.2, 0.1, 0.05, 0.02, 0.01\}$ by
5-fold cross-validated prediction; the mixture is used only if its CV
$R^2$ beats the infinitesimal $(0.5, 0.5)$ by a relative 1\% (BOLT-LMM's
threshold is not published; this is our choice), otherwise BOLT-LMM-inf
is returned. LOCO residuals
$r_g = \y - Z_{-g}^\top\hat\beta_{-g}$ replace $w_g$ in
\eqref{eq:boltinf}, and the constant is set so that the LD Score
regression intercept (Bulik-Sullivan et al.\ 2015) of the mixture
statistic equals that of BOLT-LMM-inf. In-sample LD scores use
bias-adjusted $r^2$ within 1\,Mb. When the LD scores barely vary
(coefficient of variation $< 0.2$, e.g.\ LD-free simulations) the
intercept is not identified and the medians are matched instead.

\paragraph{Blocked variational Bayes.} All cross-validation fits
(18 settings $\times$ 5 folds $= 90$ columns) or all LOCO fits ($G$
columns) run as one Gauss--Seidel iteration over SNP blocks of 128: the
block's products $Z_b^\top R$ are one GEMM, and as SNP $a$ moves by
$\Delta_a$ the products of later SNPs in the block are corrected
through the block's Gram matrix, $u_c \gets u_c - G_{ca}\Delta_a$
(per-fold Gram matrices for cross-validation columns). The sequential
part is a $B \times B \times$ columns loop (Numba), the rest is BLAS.
The posterior mean of one effect given the others is closed-form for
both priors used here (the Laplace component via truncated normals).

\subsection{LDAK-KVIK}

Following the LDAK-KVIK documentation (Hof \& Speed 2025):
(1)~structure test: among 512 SNPs spread over the genome, the mean
squared correlation between SNPs on different chromosomes,
$\mathrm{excess} = n_{\mathrm{eff}}\,\overline{r^2} - 1$; strong
structure if $\mathrm{excess} > 0.1$ with $z > 3$;
(2)~per-SNP heritabilities $h_j^2 = w_j h^2/\sum_k w_k$ with
$w_j = [f_j(1-f_j)]^{1+\alpha}$ and $\alpha$ the best of
$\{-1, -0.75, -0.5, -0.25, 0\}$ by randomized Haseman--Elston regression:
the fit score $(\sum_{i\ne j} y_iy_jK_{ij})^2/\sum_{i\ne j}K_{ij}^2$, with
$\tr \K^2$ from 32 Rademacher probes shared by every $\alpha$, so one pass
over the genotypes serves all five. $h^2$ is then fitted by REML at the
chosen $\alpha$ (LDAK-KVIK: partitioned HE, then MCMC REML; no LD thinning
here; scanning REML likelihoods over $\alpha$ is an option);
(3)~the elastic-net prior
$\gamma_j \sim p\,\mathrm{Laplace}\big(\sqrt{2p/((1-F)h_j^2)}\big) + (1-p)\,\mathcal{N}(0, F h_j^2/(1-p))$
with $(p, F)$ chosen on a 10\% hold-out among KVIK's ten pairs;
(4)~LOCO polygenic scores $P_g$ by variational Bayes; (5)~the ordinary
least-squares statistic of $\y - P_g$ on $z_j$, scaled by $\lambda$:
$\lambda = 1$ without strong structure, otherwise
$\lambda = \lambda'\sum_S T/\sum_S U$ over SNPs with $\lambda U < 6$ and
$\lambda' T < 6$, where $T$ are the statistics with ridge-prior scores
and $\lambda' = 1/c$ their GRAMMAR-Gamma calibration (the BOLT-LMM-inf
constant of the weighted model).

\subsection{Structure-aware denominator}
\label{sec:spectral}

Every statistic above, and REGENIE's and SAIGE's, scales a score by one
genome-wide constant. Under strong structure the prospective
denominator $z_j^\top(\K_{-g}+\delta I)^{-1}z_j$ is \emph{not}
proportional to $z_j^\top z_j$: a SNP aligned with the leading
(structure) eigenvectors of $\K_{-g}$ has a smaller denominator than an
unaligned SNP of equal norm, so one constant inflates unaligned SNPs and
deflates aligned ones (Section~\ref{sec:validation}). mixmogam's
extension replaces the constant by a low-rank-plus-flat approximation
on the top-$k$ eigenpairs $(U_g, \Lambda_g)$ of the SNP's own LOCO
kinship,
\begin{equation}
  D_j = c'\sigma_g^2\Big[\,\|(\Lambda_g + \delta)^{-1/2}U_g^\top z_j\|^2
  + \frac{z_j^\top z_j - \|U_g^\top z_j\|^2}{\bar\lambda_g + \delta}\Big],
  \qquad \chi^2_j = \frac{(z_j^\top w_g)^2}{D_j},
  \label{eq:spectral}
\end{equation}
with $\bar\lambda_g$ the mean of the remaining eigenvalues of
$\K_{-g}$ and $c'$ calibrated on the same 30 prospective SNPs. The width
$k$ is adaptive: it doubles from 64 to at most 512 until the spread of
$c'_j$ over the calibration SNPs falls below 3\%. The bulk of an
LD-rich kinship is not flat, and the calibration SNPs measure how much
of it the basis misses (in a coalescent sample without population
structure, $n = 1{,}500$, $k = 64$ left a 7\% spread and $k = 256$ 2\%). The LOCO
eigenbases are computed for all groups at once by randomized subspace
iteration through \eqref{eq:locoop}. A basis of the \emph{full} kinship
does not work: it contains each SNP's own LD block and reintroduces
proximal contamination into the denominator. For the mixture and
elastic-net statistics, the per-SNP factor
$\kappa_j = D_j^{\mathrm{const}}/D_j$ of the infinitesimal model is
applied before their global calibration; this transfer is a heuristic.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{BOLT-LMM-inf with the structure-aware denominator}
\label{alg:spectral}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require standardized projected blocks $Z_b$ in $G$ LOCO groups; $\y$; $(\sigma_g^2,\delta)$; width $k$
\State $W \gets$ batched PCG for $(\K_{-g}+\delta I)\,w_g = \y$, $g = 1..G$ \Comment{one LOCO pass per iteration, \eqref{eq:locoop}}
\State $\{U_g,\Lambda_g,\bar\lambda_g\} \gets$ batched randomized subspace iteration on all $\K_{-g}$
\State 30 SNPs $j$ with GRAMMAR $\chi^2<5$: $v_j \gets (\K_{-g(j)}+\delta I)^{-1}z_j$ \Comment{batched PCG}
\State $c' \gets \operatorname{mean}_j\big(\sigma_g^2 z_j^\top v_j / D_j^{(c'=1)}\big)$
\ForAll{blocks $Z_b$ of group $g$}
  \State $\mathbf{num} \gets Z_b w_g$;\quad $P \gets Z_b U_g$;\quad $D \gets c'\sigma_g^2\big[(P\circ P)(\Lambda_g{+}\delta)^{-1} + (\operatorname{rowsums}(Z_b\circ Z_b) - \operatorname{rowsums}(P\circ P))/(\bar\lambda_g{+}\delta)\big]$
  \State $\chi^2 \gets \mathbf{num}^2/D$
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Kinships}

\paragraph{Additive GRM.} Per variant $j$, genotypes are standardized
over \emph{called} samples only,
$z_{ij} = (g_{ij} - \bar g_j)/s_j$ for called $g_{ij}$ and $z_{ij}=0$
otherwise (Yang et al.\ 2010), and
$\K = \sum_j w_j\, z_j z_j^\top / \sum_j w_j$ is accumulated over SNP
blocks in \texttt{float64}. Optional weights $w_j$ implement LDAK-style
weighting; an optional SNP subset fits the null model from a marker
subsample at reduced cost.

\paragraph{IBS.} Identity-by-state similarity is accumulated as one-hot
GEMMs per block: with $H_a$ the indicator of genotype $a \in \{0,1,2\}$
over called entries, the shared-allele numerator is
$\sum_a H_a H_a^\top$ and the denominator is $C C^\top$ with $C$ the
called-indicator, so $\K_{\mathrm{IBS}} = S/C$ per pair.

\paragraph{Exact LOCO.} Because the GRM is a sum of globally
standardized outer products, the blocks accumulated for each
chromosome $\K_c = m^{-1}\sum_{j \in c} z_j z_j^\top$ satisfy $\sum_c \K_c = \K$
\emph{exactly} under a single global standardization, and the
leave-one-chromosome-out matrix is the algebraic difference
\begin{equation}
  \K_{\mathrm{loco}}(c) = \frac{m\,\K - m_c\,\K_c}{m - m_c},
\end{equation}
rescaled to mean diagonal one. No per-chromosome restandardization is
performed, hence no approximation. Windowed local/global pairs along
the genome are the same construction restricted to a sliding interval.

\section{Batched permutation thresholds}

Permuting the raw phenotype breaks the covariance the kinship models:
under relatedness or structure the permuted phenotypes are not draws
from the null model, and the threshold is anti-conservative (Abney
2015). With 4 populations at $F_{ST} = 0.3$, the v1 scheme gave a
family-wise error rate of $7.6\% \pm 0.6\%$ at a nominal 5\%
(2{,}000 parametric-null replicates, 1{,}000 permutations).
mixmogam therefore permutes the GLS-whitened null residuals
(Abney's MVNpermute construction): $r = (I - \Q\Q^\top)\Hm\y$ at the
fitted $\hat\delta$ is close to exchangeable under the null, so
\begin{equation}
  R_P = (I - \Q\Q^\top)\, r[\Pi] \in \R^{n\times B}
\end{equation}
holds $B$ draws of the whitened null. All permutations then flow
through each SNP-block GEMM at once,
\begin{equation}
  T = \widetilde\G^\top R_P \in \R^{k\times B}, \qquad
  F = \frac{T \circ T}{\mathbf{den}\,\mathrm{RSS}_{0,P}}\,\nu,
\end{equation}
and the genome-wide 5\% threshold is the 5th percentile of the
per-permutation minimum $p$. The v1 scheme remains available
(\texttt{scheme="raw"}) to reproduce old results.

\section{Two-kinship mixtures}
\label{sec:twokin}

For $\V = \sigma_{g_1}^2\K_1 + \sigma_{g_2}^2\K_2 + \sigma_e^2 I$, the
ratio $\rho = \sigma^2_{g_1}/\sigma^2_{g_2}$ is profiled on a grid of
$\log\rho$ over $[-3,3]$ (cascading refinements around the best
bracket), fitting each candidate mixture
$a\K_1 + (1{-}a)\K_2$ by the exact EMMA path of Section~\ref{sec:fit};
variance shares are reported per matrix.

\section{Missing data}

Per-SNP sample subsetting (v1's behavior) breaks block batching. The
default policy is a per-variant missingness filter followed by
mean imputation over called genotypes (EMMAX-consistent), fused into
the int8-to-float block conversion (optionally a Numba kernel);
\texttt{impute='none'} propagates NaN for analyses that prefer explicit
handling.

\section{Validation and measured performance}
\label{sec:validation}

\paragraph{Correctness.} The exact scan is certified against a float64
port of the v1 mathematics, with F statistics equal to rtol $10^{-6}$
(the oracle is \texttt{tests/\allowbreak\_reference.py}). The exact LOCO path equals a hand-built per-chromosome
EMMAX fit; the streaming LOCO operator equals the dense LOCO kinship
products; batched CG equals a dense solve; variational Bayes with a
Gaussian prior converges to the BLUP residual
$\sigma_e^2\V^{-1}\y$; both posterior means match numerical quadrature;
LD scores match a brute-force computation; whitened-residual
permutations reproduce scans of $\V^{1/2}P r$ and keep the family-wise
error at its nominal level under strong structure; the GxE statistic
equals a direct GLS F test of the full design. Without structure,
BOLT-LMM-inf agrees with the exact LOCO scan (chi-square correlation
0.996--0.9998, mean ratio 0.99--1.00), and mixmogam's LDAK-KVIK correlates
0.966 per SNP with the LDAK~6 binary.
