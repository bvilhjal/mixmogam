# Research review of mixmogam, 16 November 2026

Scope: mixmogam 2.2.0 at `b3b1bb6a` (`master`; the `v2.2.0` tag is `7f10a258`,
one commit earlier). This is a methods review rather than a code review. It
asks four questions:

1. What does each reported number estimate?
2. Is it right where the documentation says it is?
3. Do the attributions and novelty claims hold?
4. Does every quantitative claim trace to archived evidence?

The review ran five tracks:

- the lead's probe of HRATT effect sizes with relatives;
- re-verification of the "Track 5" report left by an earlier, interrupted run;
- model and estimand;
- literature and citations;
- evidence and reproducibility.

Each finding below was reproduced on the current tree with replicated seeds. The earlier run was by a different model and stopped mid-review. Its single-seed claims were re-measured, and Table 3 gives the verdicts. Nothing in the repository was modified; the 14 untracked `benchmarks/results/` directories were read only.

**Result: no Critical, 2 Major, 14 Minor, and a short list of Nits.**

- **The statistical core holds where it was validated.** That means unrelated samples, with or without structure, once PCs are in the covariates. There, HRATT matches the exact LMM in effect size, null λ_GC and QTL χ².
- **The reproducibility system is in good shape.** 96 traced claims reproduce, all 22 report tables regenerate byte-identically, and every live manifest hash matches.
- **Major 1:** with `relatedness=` and the default random cross-fit folds, HRATT reports effects that are 6–27% too small in family-rich samples. The test itself stays calibrated, and nothing warns.
- **Major 2:** one arm of a "plausibly new" design choice is REGENIE's published approximate LOCO scheme, and it is uncited.

The review was report-only when written. **The findings were then addressed the same day; see "Remediation" at the end.**

## Severity scale

- **Critical**: silently wrong numbers under realistic use.
- **Major**: wrong under specific, plausible conditions, or a misattribution a reader would rely on.
- **Minor**: robustness, diagnostics, documentation precision.
- **Nit**: cosmetic.

## Environment

- Interpreter: the `ldpred3-accelerate` env (CPython 3.14), with mixmogam 2.2.0 installed editable.
- Every probe ran with BLAS pinned to one thread and an isolated `NUMBA_CACHE_DIR`.
- Evidence: `research-review-mixmogam-2026-11-16-evidence/`, beside the repository and not in it. Its contents are listed in the appendix.

## Findings

### Major

#### M1. HRATT effects are attenuated under `relatedness=` in family-rich samples

**Location.**
- The effect estimate is `beta_z = rsU["num"] / rsU["zz"] * sy`, with no effect calibration:
  - kappa_g path: `mixmogam/twostep.py:2314`;
  - weighted kappa path: `mixmogam/twostep.py:2302`;
  - GLS path: `2308` (`gls["num"] / gls["den"]`);
  - dosage step 2: `mixmogam/steps.py:435`.
- Documentation:
  - `README.md:66-67` says effects are "within 2% of the population slopes".
  - `docs/design.md:430-431` presents cross-fitting as "an alternative to LDAK-KVIK's effect calibration".
  - `docs/quickstart.md:276-278` and the `hratt` docstring (`twostep.py:1520-1527`) describe the trade-off of `crossfit_folds` only in terms of χ².

**Mechanism.**
1. The cross-fitted LOCO predictor for sample i is trained on folds that contain i's relatives. It therefore predicts y_i partly from their phenotypes, acting as a kinship smoother.
2. Those phenotypes carry the tested QTL through Mendelian sharing at the tested locus (covariance 2φ). Removing chromosome g's variants from the predictor does not remove this path.
3. Subtracting the predictor removes part of the QTL signal from y while the genotype is left untransformed, so the slope shrinks toward zero. This is the GRAMMAR bias.
4. kappa_g rescales the variance, so z and p stay calibrated: (β/se)² = χ², and the se shrinks with β.
5. GLS whitens the residual but not the predictor's absorption, so it is attenuated too.
6. Family folds keep relatives out of a sample's training folds and remove the bias. The cost is the power loss already documented.
7. Without `relatedness=`, the relatives trip the structure test (λ 1.15–1.44). λ then multiplies β, which partly undoes the shrinkage. Declaring the relatives therefore gives worse effects (0.73–0.83×) than not declaring them (0.95–0.97×), though the undeclared run has inflated null λ_GC.

**Evidence.** `lead/l1_relatedness_beta.py` (12 seeds) and `lead/l2_dose_response.py` (10 seeds).

- Panels have unlinked loci with Mendelian transmission and 10 QTLs on chromosome 5; chromosome 6 is purely null; background h² = 0.4.
- The true per-allele effect is the simulated standardized effect divided by sd(G).
- Values are means over seeds, with the MCSE in parentheses.

Two earlier measurements agree:
- The interrupted run's 10-seed sibship probe found −17% to −19% for random folds against family folds.
- Its h² = 0.5, c² = 0.25 probe gave 0.734 against 0.966 for exact.

**Precedent.**
- GRAMMAR's effects are known to be biased toward zero. GenABEL's own code says GRAMMAR-GC's "estimates of regression coefficients are biased (the same as in 'raw' GRAMMAR)".
- GRAMMAR-Gamma divides β by one global γ.
- LDAK-KVIK computes a genome-wide `Effect_Calibration` in step 1 (`quickpred.c`) and applies it in step 2 (`singled.c`).
- Mefford et al. (bioRxiv 10.1101/517821) found exactly this for cross-validated predictors with 200 ten-person pedigrees: "cvBLUP adjusted analyses and the results from BOLT have biased effect estimates under the alternative … over-adjustment for family structure".

**Severity.** Major. The effects are silently biased in family-based cohorts, isolates and sibling designs, and effects feed meta-analysis, MR and PGS weights. The p-values are unaffected, and biobank-like mixes (≤ 20% of samples in pairs) are not measurably affected.

**Options (a decision for the user).**
- (a) A per-group effect calibration, either LDAK-KVIK's slope at random SNPs or the GRAMMAR-Gamma form, validated against Table 1.
- (b) Effects from family folds and tests from random folds. This needs a second stage-1 fit, or family folds as the default when relatives are dense.
- (c) At least, document the bias and warn when the related fraction or family size is large.

#### M2. The "drop-share" arm of a novelty claim is REGENIE's approximate LOCO

**Location.**
- `docs/design.md:337-351` and `425-428` (novelty item 4).
- `report/mixmogam_report.tex:855` and `2436-2441`.

**Claim in the docs.** Dropping a group's share of one genome-wide fit, instead of refitting per group, appears with no citation as one arm of a "plausibly new" structure-conditional switch.

**Source.** REGENIE's preprint (bioRxiv 10.1101/2020.06.19.162354 v2, Supplementary Methods) describes the same scheme:

> "for each chromosome, we compute the whole-genome predictions by setting the entries … corresponding to blocks in the chromosome to 0. This approach is less computationally burdensome than a more exact LOCO scheme … We have found that it works well in practice."

Further evidence:
- regenie's source (`Data.cpp`, commit 65ec67f) does the same: `pred.col(chr - 1) -= predictions[0].col(chr_ctr)`.
- The published paper (Nat Genet 53:1097) reports R² = 0.99932 between the approximate and exact LOCO schemes for quantitative traits. Its simulations always included the top PC.

**What is new.** mixmogam's own result (null-chromosome λ_GC 1.5–4.4 at Fst 0.15 without PCs, against 0.9–1.1 when refitted) is a genuine addition to REGENIE's validation. It should be stated that way.

**Related precedent, also understated.**
- REGENIE cross-fits both levels of its step-1 predictor, not only level 0 (`design.md:296`, `397-399`).
- The BOLT-LMM preprint (10.1101/007799) already notes that "there should formally be a different denominator for each chromosome, but all are approximately equal". That bears on novelty item 3, the per-group retrospective factor.

**Severity.** Major as an attribution issue in a manuscript-style report.

### Minor

**m1. Binary one-step effects are biased upward for low-frequency risk alleles.**
- Location: `twostep.py:2875`; README:71 says "within 3%".
- Measured (10 seeds, against the same model's MLE, prevalence 5%, f = 0.05):
  - β = 0.2: +7.6%;
  - β = 0.4: +17.6%;
  - β = 0.7: +30.6%.
- The effect shrinks with frequency and prevalence and is about −1% to −3% at 50% prevalence.
- Analytic check: for a rare allele and a rare outcome, U/J tends to e^β − 1. For β = 0.4 that gives 17.4% analytically against 17.6% measured.
- The README's figure holds for its archived cell. That figure had MCSE 4–9% and did not test low-frequency risk alleles.
- Consequence: `gwas_by_group` with binary HRATT reports a spurious b1 − b0 when prevalence differs between groups. Analytically: −0.103 log-OR at true OR 2, f = 0.05 and prevalence 5% against 20%.
- The labels "one-step approximation" and "experimental" are present, so this is a precision issue.

**m2. Strong structure, no PCs and an ancestry-correlated environment destabilize λ.**
- Location: `twostep.py:2759-2805`; warning text at `1337` and `2083-2089`.
- Measured (8 seeds, 3 populations, Fst 0.15, 0.10 environmental shift):
  - λ ranged 0.78–1.26;
  - null λ_GC ranged 0.60–1.17 (mean 0.92) against exact's 0.92–1.14;
  - HRATT's QTL χ² was 0.92 of exact's.
- At Fst 0.05: null λ_GC 0.86 (exact 0.93) and β −14.3% (3.4).
- The cause is that λ ≈ λ′ inherits λ′'s dataset-level miscalibration. More calibration SNPs or a fixed h² did not help.
- Without the environment term, or with PCs, the path is fine.
- The warning says "null lambda_GC still reached 1.01-1.13". It should say the result can be deflated or inflated and that effects shrink. PCs are already required.

**m3. λ multiplies quantitative and weighted β, but the docs say otherwise.**
- `beta_z = lam * …` at `twostep.py:2320` and `steps.py:438`.
- `design.md:569`, `twostep.py:1655` and `report.tex:779` call the effects "weighted least-squares slopes".
- Under strong structure, the λ scaling improves the mean bias but adds a per-dataset factor that the reported se does not carry (per-seed SD 0.09–0.16 against 0.02–0.04 for exact).
- Binary effects (`2875`) are not λ-scaled.

**m4. Convergence of ldpred3-* stage-1 backends is not surfaced.**
- `stage1.py:571-572` and `676-677` hard-code `cv_converged` and `loco_converged` to True.
- The R-hat check (`650-658`) runs before the leave-group refit chains are added. In a probe with refit chains at R-hat up to 1.44 (6 of 15 above 1.1), `fast_max_rhat_h2` was 1.09 and no warning fired.
- lassosum2's `.converged` and `.max_kkt_violation` are discarded (`362-363`). Two of 6 default fits returned False.
- `quickstart.md:79-81` tells users to check these flags.

**m5. KING-auto under structure drops full sibs, but the docs say only 2nd- and 3rd-degree relatives.**
- Code: `_relatedness.py:524-534`, `991-1025`.
- Measured (10 seeds, 400 sibships of four, 2 populations, Fst 0.08, PCs supplied):
  - floor 0.236–0.258;
  - 14–86% of sib pairs dropped (mean about 54%);
  - null λ_GC +0.047 (0.011) against the pedigree run.
- The runtime warning correctly names 1st-degree relatives.
- `quickstart.md:228-230`, `README.md:137-138` and the `hratt` docstring name only 2nd- and 3rd-degree relatives.

**m6. bolt-inf and bolt never warn on a large `calibration_cv`, but HRATT does.**
- Measured (8 seeds, 4 populations, Fst 0.2, no PCs, true null chromosome):
  - overall FPR at p < 0.01 was 1.21%;
  - by loading quintile it ran 2.25% (Q1) against 0.31% (Q5);
  - `calibration_cv` 0.167, with no warning.
- The redistribution is documented in `design.md:682-727`. The silence, and the lack of a threshold for "large", are not.
- Same setting: bolt-inf effects are +4.8% (1.6) against exact −0.6% (`twostep.py:828`).
- `gwas(method="auto")` chooses constant-denominator bolt-inf above 5,000 samples.

**m7. Rare binary variants carried by whole sibships exceed the documented range.**
- Measured (10 seeds × 5,000 variants per pattern) when all four sibs carry in each of two families:
  - 3.64× [3.13, 4.21] at 1e-3;
  - 5.2× [3.4, 7.6] at 1e-4.
- The quickstart's 1.2–2.3× (about 3.5× at 1e-4) holds for Mendelian transmission patterns.
- Every hit below 1e-4 was flagged by `few_carrier_families`.

**m8. The weighted retrospective variance assumes weights that do not depend on the tested genotype.**
- With a per-allele participation log-OR of −0.1 at f = 0.05, the true null variance is 1.18 times the modelled one, by exact quadrature. The error is first order.
- The exchangeability assumption is stated, but weights built from a genotype-based participation model are not discussed.

**m9. `_hratt_lambda`'s fixed point is unbounded.**
- Location: `twostep.py:2796-2801`.
- With an empty selection set it raises `ZeroDivisionError` at `2798`.
- On a pathological all-duplicated panel it returned λ = 70.1 silently: λ_GC 186 on a null trait.
- `design.md:285` lists "on failure: none".
- se is NaN for a constructed variant with an exactly zero score (`2309`, `2315`, `2320-2322`).
- Not reachable with ordinary data.

**m10. Some evidence ranges are stated too narrowly.**

## What would change these conclusions, and what was not verified

- **M1** would shrink to a footnote if the cohorts mixmogam targets have at most 20% of samples in pairs (Table 2, first row).
  - It needs a fix only where families are dense.
  - All panels here are small (n ≤ 2,400, 3,000 unlinked loci). The magnitude depends on h², c², family size and n, and was not measured at biobank scale.
  - A GRAMMAR-Gamma or LDAK-style effect calibration was not prototyped.
- **Literature.** Paywalled texts were read through abstracts and preprints only: Hof & Speed 2025, Svishcheva 2012, Campos 2023, and the published Mefford paper. LDAK-KVIK's `Effect_Calibration` was read in source and was not run.
- **Prior-art search** for items 3 and 4 is not exhaustive, because the bioRxiv tool has no keyword search.
- **The binary bias (m1)** was measured on n = 3,000 panels; the analytic limit supports its direction and size.

## Remediation (16 November 2026)

Each finding was weighed again before acting. Both Majors and most Minors were addressed in code or documentation. The rest are recorded below as decisions or as findings left alone, with the reason. The changes are uncommitted in the working tree as version 2.3.0.dev1.

### Package changes

1. **Family cross-fit folds by default (M1).** `relatedness_options["crossfit_folds"]` now defaults to `"family"`: with relatives, each family goes whole to one fold.
   - Family folds follow the evidence rule of the relatives refit they replace (a supplied pair, or KING-auto's trigger), so a panel without relatives keeps its ordinary folds and results.
   - With `relatedness="auto"`, families also link the pre-floor pairs above the trigger's n-aware cutoff before its 0.0884 cap. Under structure the noise floor can drop full sibs from Φ (m5), and those sibs would otherwise be split across folds. The capped cutoff is not used for linking: with 1,000 KING variants it let 115 noise pairs chain families into a 432-sample component, which forced random folds.
   - Binary traits balance cases across the family folds (`_family_folds(strata=)`, invariant to swapping the labels).
   - `stage1="ldpred3-inf"` now uses family folds too. Its block-exact LOCO only makes the relatives refit unnecessary; it does not stop the leak.
   - `extra["relatedness"]["crossfit_leak"]` records the mean squared relatedness to relatives in other folds. Random folds, whether requested or the fallback when a family is too large, warn above 0.05.
2. **A retrospective variance for the GLS correction.** `"gls"` now tests its GLS-weighted score z′b (b = Pw) against the score's variance given the phenotypes, σ²b′Φb, with σ² estimated as for kappa_g. This is MASTOR's construction.
   - `relatedness_options["gls_variance"]` selects it: `"retrospective"` is the new default, and `"prospective"` gives the earlier z′Pz.
   - Effects, the GLS slopes, are unchanged.
   - Step-1 files saved before this change keep the prospective variance.
3. **LDpred3 convergence (m4).**
   - lassosum2 fits record ldpred3's KKT check, and a fit above 10× its tolerance counts as unconverged.

### Why family folds rather than an effect calibration

Three options were open for M1:
- (a) a per-group effect calibration in the style of GRAMMAR-Gamma or LDAK-KVIK;
- (b) family folds;
- (c) a warning only.

Option (a) was rejected on two grounds:
1. **The slope is not exact here.** LDAK-KVIK's slope of marginal on residual effects at random SNPs gives the exact correction for GRAMMAR's in-sample residual, σₑ²V⁻¹y. A cross-fitted predictor that reads relatives' phenotypes is not of that form. In a family-mean model of sibships of four the slope over-corrected by 1.5% without a household effect and by 5.5% with one (analytic).
2. **It treats the symptom.** Cross-fitting assumes independent units, and with relatives the units are families.

Family folds remove the leak at its source. They cost power with kappa_g (Table 4), and the GLS correction recovers it (Tables 4 and 5).

Table 4. Fold design against relatedness correction, n = 1,600 in 400 sibships of four, 12 seeds per cell (`lead/l3_fold_correction_grid.py`; the GLS rows ran before item 2, so they use the prospective variance).

| method | β/true | QTL χ² | null λ_GC | β/true, c² 0.25 | QTL χ² | null λ_GC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| exact LMM | 0.979 | 22.5 | 1.023 | 1.043 | 30.8 | 1.149 |
| random folds + kappa (old default) | 0.800 | 20.1 | 1.059 | 0.802 | 21.7 | 1.025 |
| family folds + kappa (new default) | 0.972 | 18.1 | 1.037 | 1.049 | 16.4 | 0.985 |
| random folds + GLS | 0.774 | 19.2 | 1.046 | 0.817 | 22.7 | 1.032 |
| family folds + GLS | 0.972 | 21.1 | 1.039 | 1.039 | 27.1 | 0.945 |

Two further runs fit the same picture:
- **Binary trait** (20% prevalence, c² 0.25): random-fold one-step effects were 0.918 (0.008) of the family-fold ones, with QTL χ² 10.1 against 8.9.
- **`ldpred3-inf`** (c² 0, 6 seeds, `lead/l4_ldpred3_inf_leak.py`): 0.817 (0.025) with random folds, 0.986 (0.029) with family folds, 0.985 for exact.

Table 5. The GLS variance with family folds, sibships of four with household c² = 0.3 (n = 1,600, 12 seeds, 800 null SNPs per seed; `lead/l5_retro_gls_proto.py`). Ascertainment kept the 400 of 1,200 families with the highest mean phenotype.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| unascertained | kappa | 1.015 | 1.003 | 13.7 |

Why prospective GLS is conservative here: with a household effect, the sib correlation approaches 0.5, which the model s_e I + s_f R cannot exceed, so s_f sits at its clip (0.95 of the total). The retrospective variance is valid for any weight vector and is unaffected. The package implementation matches the prototype's χ² to 7e-13 (`lead/l6_check_impl.py`).
