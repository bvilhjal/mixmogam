\title{Calibrating mixed-model association under population structure:\\
exact LOCO, two-step statistics and a structure-aware denominator}
\author{mixmogam contributors}
\date{October 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Mixed-model association scans now test each SNP against a kinship built
without its own chromosome (leave one chromosome out, LOCO), and the
methods used for large cohorts compute such scans in two steps. They
scale a retrospective score by a single genome-wide calibration
constant. We describe mixmogam~2, which implements exact LOCO EMMAX,
BOLT-LMM-inf, BOLT-LMM and LDAK-KVIK on a shared streaming LOCO
operator, and we evaluate calibration per SNP rather than genome-wide.
On 1,307 \emph{Arabidopsis thaliana} RegMap accessions with a null
chromosome, single-constant statistics are anti-conservative for SNPs
that load weakly on the leading kinship eigenvectors and conservative
for SNPs that load strongly on them. The reference LDAK-KVIK program
gives $\lgc = 1.21$ and a false-positive rate of 1.67\% at $\alpha = 1\%$
in the lowest loading quintile, against 0.92 and 0.78\% in the highest,
while its genome-wide $\lgc$ is 1.04. A denominator built on each SNP's
own LOCO eigenbasis restores flat calibration (1.04 and 1.03). In
simulation, a kinship alone absorbs a strong environment that tracks
ancestry only partly: exact LOCO gives $\lgc = 1.27$, and null SNPs in
the top 1\% of loading on the environment reach mean $\chi^2 = 2.27$.
One principal component as a fixed covariate removes the residual (1.11
and 1.13). The non-LOCO scan's apparent calibration (0.98) is the same
residual offset by proximal-contamination deflation. We also describe
two traps in simulation design, a confounder built from the tested SNPs
and LD blocks cut through LD, and their remedies.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Linear mixed models are the standard tool against confounding by
population structure and relatedness in genome-wide association studies
\citep{kang2008,kang2010,yang2010,lippert2011,zhou2012,yang2014}. The
confounders are the environment and the genetic background, both of
which track ancestry \citep{vilhjalmsson2013}; principal components
\citep{price2006} and mixed models \citep{price2010,sul2018} address
them in different ways. Two refinements are now routine. First, testing
a SNP against a kinship that contains it lets the polygenic term absorb
part of the SNP's own effect (proximal contamination), so scans leave
the tested chromosome out of the kinship \citep{listgarten2012,yang2014}.
Second, for large cohorts the exact statistic is replaced by a two-step
one. The first step fits a LOCO polygenic model without forming the
kinship; the second tests each SNP against the LOCO residual with a
retrospective score, scaled by one genome-wide constant
\citep{loh2015,mbatchou2021,hof2025}.

One constant is exact only if every SNP's prospective denominator is
proportional to its squared norm (Section~\ref{sec:twostep}). Under
population structure it is not, because a SNP aligned with the leading
kinship eigenvectors has a smaller prospective denominator than an
unaligned SNP of equal norm. The BOLT-LMM authors tested their method
on human data only, and noted that treating this denominator as nearly
constant may fail in plant and animal data \citep{loh2015}. A genome-wide
$\lgc$ averages over SNPs and need not show the problem. Our estimand is
therefore the null distribution of each SNP's statistic given its
loading on the leading kinship eigenvectors, summarized by $\lgc$ and the
false-positive rate (FPR) within loading quintiles.

This report makes four contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item an implementation of exact LOCO EMMAX and of the two-step
        statistics of BOLT-LMM and LDAK-KVIK on one streaming LOCO
        operator (Section~\ref{sec:methods});
  \item per-SNP calibration on real \emph{A.\ thaliana} genotypes
        \citep{horton2012} and on simulated structure, including the
        reference LDAK-KVIK program (Sections~\ref{sec:res-at} and
        \ref{sec:res-sim});
  \item a structure-aware denominator that restores per-SNP calibration
        for BOLT-LMM-inf (Section~\ref{sec:spectral});
  \item a simulation study with a confounder that is independent of the
        tested SNPs and with LD-split blocks. It compares LOCO with
        non-LOCO, with and without a principal-component covariate
        (Sections~\ref{sec:res-s2} and \ref{sec:res-blocks}).
\end{enumerate}
mixmogam's first version provided the multi-locus mixed model
\citep{segura2012}; the present engine replaces it. Full derivations
and pseudocode are in the technical documentation,
\texttt{docs/\allowbreak methods.pdf}.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Model and exact LOCO scan}

Let $n$ be the number of samples, $\X \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times q}$ the
covariates (with an intercept) and $\K$ a genomic relationship matrix
built from standardized genotypes \citep{yang2010}. The model is
\begin{equation}
  \y = \X\beta + u + e, \qquad u \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_g^2\K), \qquad
  e \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_e^2\I_n),
  \label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
with $\delta = \sigma_e^2/\sigma_g^2$ and $\V = \sigma_g^2(\K + \delta\I_n)$.
Variance components are fitted by REML from one eigendecomposition of
$\K$ \citep{kang2008}. Write $\Hm = \V^{-1/2}$, $Q$ for an orthonormal
basis of $\Hm\X$, and $r = (\I - QQ^\top)\Hm\y$. For a SNP $g$ with
$\tilde g = (\I - QQ^\top)\Hm g$, the EMMAX statistic \citep{kang2010} is
\begin{equation}
  F(g) = \frac{(\tilde g^\top r)^2/\tilde g^\top\tilde g}
              {\big(r^\top r - (\tilde g^\top r)^2/\tilde g^\top\tilde g\big)/(n-q-1)}
  \sim F_{1,\,n-q-1}.
  \label{eq:emmax}
\end{equation}
All SNPs of a block are transformed by matrix products, so the scan is
dense linear algebra without a per-SNP solve. For LOCO, SNPs are grouped
by chromosome (more than 25 chromosomes are merged into 25 contiguous
groups), and the kinship of group $g$ is obtained by subtraction,
\begin{equation}
  \K_{-g} \propto \sum_{j} \z_j\z_j^\top - \sum_{j \in g} \z_j\z_j^\top ,
  \label{eq:loco}
\end{equation}
with $\z_j$ the globally standardized genotypes and the result rescaled
to mean diagonal one. The variance components are refitted for each
group, and the SNPs of the group are scanned with~\eqref{eq:emmax}.

\subsection{Two-step statistics and the constant-ratio assumption}
\label{sec:twostep}

BOLT-LMM-inf \citep{loh2015} estimates $(\sigma_g^2, \delta)$ once and
solves $\w_g = (\K_{-g} + \delta\I)^{-1}\y$ for every group by
conjugate gradients, never forming $\K_{-g}$. A SNP $\z_j$ in group $g$
gets the retrospective statistic
\begin{equation}
  \chi^2_j = \frac{(\z_j^\top\w_g)^2}{c\,(\z_j^\top\z_j/n)\,\|\w_g\|^2},
  \label{eq:boltinf}
\end{equation}
where the constant $c$ is the mean, over 30 random SNPs, of the ratio
that makes~\eqref{eq:boltinf} equal the prospective statistic
$(\z_j^\top\V_{-g}^{-1}\y)^2/\z_j^\top\V_{-g}^{-1}\z_j$. One constant can
match the prospective statistic for every SNP only if
\begin{equation}
  \z_j^\top(\K_{-g} + \delta\I)^{-1}\z_j \;\propto\; \z_j^\top\z_j
  \quad\text{for all } j.
  \label{eq:assumption}
\end{equation}
Write $\K_{-g} = \U\Lambda\U^\top$. The left side of
\eqref{eq:assumption} is $\sum_k (\U_k^\top\z_j)^2/(\lambda_k + \delta)$,
so a SNP that loads on the large leading eigenvalues (structure) has a
small denominator, and a SNP that loads on the bulk has a large one. A
constant fitted to the average is then too small for unaligned SNPs
(inflation) and too large for aligned ones (deflation). BOLT-LMM
\citep{loh2015} replaces $\w_g$ by the residual of a two-Gaussian
mixture prior fitted by variational Bayes and matches the LD Score
regression intercept \citep{bulikSullivan2015}. LDAK-KVIK \citep{hof2025}
uses an elastic-net prior and a scaling rule that is triggered by a
structure test. Both keep one constant. mixmogam's re-implementations
and their departures from the reference programs are documented in
\texttt{docs/methods.pdf}.

\subsection{Structure-aware denominator}
\label{sec:spectral}

mixmogam replaces the constant by a per-SNP denominator on the top-$k$
eigenpairs $(\U_g, \Lambda_g)$ of the SNP's own LOCO kinship, with the
rest of the spectrum treated as flat at its mean $\bar\lambda_g$:
\begin{equation}
  D_j = c'\sigma_g^2\left[\big\|(\Lambda_g + \delta)^{-1/2}\U_g^\top\z_j\big\|^2
        + \frac{\z_j^\top\z_j - \|\U_g^\top\z_j\|^2}{\bar\lambda_g + \delta}\right],
  \qquad \chi^2_j = \frac{(\z_j^\top\w_g)^2}{D_j},
  \label{eq:spectral}
\end{equation}
with $c'$ calibrated on the same 30 SNPs. The basis must come from the
LOCO kinship: a basis of the full kinship contains each SNP's own LD
block and reintroduces proximal contamination into the denominator. The
LOCO eigenbases of all groups are computed together by randomized
subspace iteration through one streaming operator. The archived
calibration runs used $k = 64$; the current default doubles $k$ from 64
to at most 512 until the spread of $c'_j$ over the calibration SNPs is
below 3\%. For the mixture and elastic-net statistics, the per-SNP factor
of the infinitesimal model is applied before their own calibration;
this transfer is a heuristic.

\subsection{Principal components under strong environmental stratification}

Under~\eqref{eq:model} the variance of $\y$ along an eigenvector of $\K$
is $\sigma_g^2\lambda_k + \sigma_e^2$, the same function of the
eigenvalue for every direction. An environment that moves the phenotype
along the leading (ancestry) eigenvector by a large amount therefore
cannot be represented, and REML compensates by inflating $\sigma_g^2$.
The standard remedy adds the top principal components of $\K$ to $\X$
\citep{price2006,price2010}; we evaluate it with one component.

\subsection{Implementation}

The two-step methods hold standardized, covariate-projected SNP blocks
that never straddle a LOCO group. One pass over the blocks applies every
$\K_{-g}$ to its own column of a matrix, so the LOCO solves, the
calibration solves and the LOCO eigenbases share matrix products.
Variational Bayes for the mixture priors runs all cross-validation folds
or all LOCO groups at once. Variance components for large $n$ come from
deflated stochastic Lanczos quadrature on the streaming operator. Scans
default to single precision and fits to double precision. A fitted model
and its cached fit must not reference each other: such a cycle kept
every LOCO group's kinship and eigendecomposition in memory until
Python's cyclic garbage collector ran, about 8\,GB per exact LOCO scan
at $n = 4{,}000$. The model now stores the fit without the back
reference.

\subsection{Evaluation designs}
\label{sec:designs}

\paragraph{Per-SNP calibration.} The genetic value is built from every
chromosome except the last: a polygenic component with $h^2 = 0.4$ on
standardized genotypes plus 10 QTLs with $h^2 = 0.1$. The SNPs on the last
chromosome are then null, and their LOCO kinship is the generating one.
Each null SNP is assigned the share of its squared norm that falls in the
top 10 eigenvectors of the kinship, and null SNPs are binned into
quintiles of this loading. We report $\lgc$ (median $\chi^2_1$ over
0.455) and the FPR at $p < 0.01$ per quintile, as means over six
replicate phenotypes. The data are (i)~the \emph{A.\ thaliana} RegMap
panel \citep{horton2012}: 1,307 inbred accessions, every fourth SNP
with minor allele count at least 10 (53,167 SNPs on 5 chromosomes, 13,209
of them on the null chromosome 5); and (ii)~simulated LD-free genotypes,
$n = 1{,}300$ and $m = 20{,}000$ on 5 chromosomes, without structure and
with 4 populations at $F_{ST} = 0.3$. On RegMap the reference LDAK-KVIK
program (LDAK version 6, \texttt{--kvik-step1} and \texttt{--kvik-step2}
with default settings) was run on the same phenotypes.

\paragraph{Simulation study.} Genotypes come from the coalescent with
recombination \citep{kelleher2016,baumdicker2022}: $N_e = 10^4$,
recombination and mutation rates $10^{-8}$ per base pair per
generation, minor allele frequency above 1\%. Each LD block is its own
``chromosome'', so a locus is a block. In scenarios S1 and S3 one
simulated segment is cut into $m/200$ blocks of 100--400 SNPs by the
optimal LD split of \citet{prive2022}, which minimizes the $r^2$ leaking
across block boundaries \citep[cf.][]{berisa2016}. Traits have
heritability $h^2$, half from an infinitesimal background over all SNPs
and half from QTLs: S1 has $n = 800$, $m = 20{,}000$, $h^2 = 0.5$ and 20
QTLs; S3 has $n = 4{,}000$, $m = 50{,}000$, $h^2 = 0.5$ and 30 QTLs. Power
and false loci are counted per block at the 5\% Bonferroni threshold;
false loci are significant blocks without a QTL. Three seeds per
scenario.

\paragraph{Confounding by environment (S2).} The sample comes from two
demes that split 400 generations ago (each and their ancestor of size
$10^4$), 400 diploids from each. Every one of 100 blocks of 200 SNPs is
an independent coalescent replicate, so blocks are unlinked. The trait
is the S1 construction with $h^2 = 0.25$ and 10 QTLs, plus an
environment equal to the standardized deme indicator, which carries 50\%
of the liability variance. The environment correlates with every SNP
that drifted between the demes, but no tested SNP causes it. Null SNPs
are those in blocks without a QTL. We report their $\lgc$ and their mean
$\chi^2$ in the bottom half and in the top 1\% of loading on the
environment (squared correlation with it). As a negative control, the
superseded design put the confounder on the leading eigenvector of the
tested SNPs' own kinship in a panmictic sample. That axis is a weighted
sum of the tested SNPs, so the SNPs that load on it most carry the
confounder themselves.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Per-SNP calibration on \emph{A.\ thaliana}}
\label{sec:res-at}

Exact LOCO EMMAX is flat across the loading quintiles on RegMap
($\lgc$ 1.04 in the lowest and highest quintile, FPR 0.96\% and 0.98\%;
Table~\ref{tab:calibration}, Figure~\ref{fig:calibration}b). Every
single-constant statistic shows a gradient. BOLT-LMM-inf goes from
$\lgc = 1.19$ (FPR 1.66\%) to 0.86 (0.56\%), mixmogam's LDAK-KVIK from
1.16 (1.44\%) to 0.85 (0.49\%), and the reference LDAK-KVIK program from
1.21 (1.67\%) to 0.92 (0.78\%). Their genome-wide $\lgc$ are 0.99--1.04.
The reference program detected strong structure in every replicate and
scaled its statistics by 1.14--1.39; its per-SNP $\chi^2$ correlates
0.958 with exact LOCO EMMAX and 0.966 with mixmogam's re-implementation.
The structure-aware denominator gives 1.04 and 1.03 in the extreme
quintiles (FPR 0.97\% and 0.99\%) and correlates 0.999 with the exact
statistic.

\subsection{Simulated structure}
\label{sec:res-sim}

Without structure every method is flat ($\lgc$ 0.98--1.04 in every
quintile). With four populations at $F_{ST} = 0.3$ the gradient is
steeper than on RegMap (Figure~\ref{fig:calibration}a): BOLT-LMM-inf
goes from 1.34 to 0.64 (FPR 2.45\% to 0.11\%), BOLT-LMM from 1.35 to
0.70, and LDAK-KVIK from 1.37 to 0.73 (2.92\% to 0.11\%), while exact
LOCO stays between 0.92 and 1.05. The structure-aware denominator brings
BOLT-LMM-inf to 1.05 and 1.01 in the extreme quintiles. Each two-step
result reports the spread of its calibration ratio over the 30
calibration SNPs, which flags the problem: 0.5\% without structure,
23\% at $F_{ST} = 0.3$ and 13\% on RegMap, against 0.4\%, 0.3\% and 3\%
with the structure-aware denominator. Mean $\chi^2$ at the QTLs differed
by a few percent across methods.

\subsection{Confounding by an environment that tracks ancestry}
\label{sec:res-s2}

The two-deme samples have $F_{ST} = 0.020$ (Hudson's estimator;
\citealp{bhatia2013}). The leading GRM eigenvalue is 16--17, against
about 3 for LD, and PC1 has $r^2 = 0.97$ with deme. The environment
inflates the plain linear model to $\lgc = 11.8$ and makes every null
block significant (Table~\ref{tab:s2}). The mixed models remove most of
this, but not all.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{The confounder no longer favours non-LOCO.} Under the
        superseded design, null SNPs in the top 1\% of loading had mean
        $\chi^2$ 9.8 with LOCO and 6.0 without, which the overall $\lgc$
        (1.39 and 0.98) hid (Figure~\ref{fig:s2}a). With an independent
        confounder, both show the same mild gradient, twice their
        bottom-half mean (Figure~\ref{fig:s2}b).
  \item \emph{A kinship alone absorbs the environment only partly.}
        REML inflates the pseudo-heritability to 0.66 against a
        simulated genetic share of 0.125. Null SNPs keep a loading
        gradient, and exact LOCO sits at $\lgc = 1.27$ (BOLT-LMM-inf
        1.28). With PC1 as a covariate the gradient vanishes (1.10 in
        the bottom half, 1.13 in the top 1\%) and $\lgc$ drops to 1.11.
  \item \emph{Non-LOCO's 0.98 is not better calibration.} It is the
        same residual, offset by the proximal-contamination deflation
        that the non-LOCO scan also shows without structure (S1: 0.95
        against 1.25 for LOCO; Table~\ref{tab:blocks}).
  \item \emph{At the locus level the residual stays below the
        Bonferroni threshold.} Every mixed model has at most one false
        locus per scan. Power at $n = 800$ is low for all of them: 6 of 30
        QTLs were found with LOCO and 4 without.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{LOCO, power and LD-split blocks}
\label{sec:res-blocks}

LOCO raises locus-level power in S3 from 0.33 to 0.51
(Table~\ref{tab:blocks}). Its $\lgc$ (1.65) exceeds the non-LOCO
value (0.91) because the infinitesimal background puts true signal on
every SNP. LOCO keeps that signal, while the non-LOCO scan is deflated
by proximal contamination. The same background makes some ``false''
loci real associations; at $n = 4{,}000$ LOCO detects them.

Cutting the coalescent segment every 200 SNPs, as the superseded design
did, splits strong LD. The largest $r^2$ between the last and first 20
SNPs of neighbouring blocks had median 1.0 and exceeded 0.5 at 99--100\%
of boundaries, and every S1 false locus under those cuts was a QTL tag
in the neighbouring block. On the same data, the LD split lowers the
$r^2$ leaking across boundaries by 13--14\% and removes all S1 false loci
(Table~\ref{tab:blocks}). At $n = 4{,}000$ it removes 6 of LOCO's 17
false loci in the first seed, but 9 of the remaining 11 still sit next to
a causal block. In this constant-size coalescent, $r^2 > 0.8$ reaches
more than 1,000 SNPs between low-frequency variants, so no cut avoids
every strong pair; for the same reason, the bigsnpr default of
forbidding cuts through pairs with $r^2 > 0.3$ was infeasible.

\subsection{Large samples and computation}

At $n = 10{,}000$ the K-free variance-component fit (stochastic Lanczos
quadrature on the streaming kinship) reproduced the exact fit's $\delta$
to 0.09\% (0.91724 against 0.91639), with no $n \times n$ matrix formed.
A scan on a truncated top-$k$ spectrum with a flat tail, which mixmogam
had used for large $n$, was anti-conservative ($\lgc = 1.30$ at
$n = 10{,}000$ with $k = 1{,}024$); it is no longer a default, and large
samples use the two-step statistics. Table~\ref{tab:compute} lists
measured wall times.

\section{Discussion}

Single-constant calibration is a statement about the average SNP, and
under population structure the average hides a gradient. On real
\emph{A.\ thaliana} genotypes the reference LDAK-KVIK program and
mixmogam's BOLT-LMM-inf are anti-conservative for SNPs that load weakly
on structure, about 1.7 times the nominal FPR at $\alpha = 1\%$, and
conservative for SNPs that load strongly on it, while their genome-wide
$\lgc$ looks normal. The gradient follows from~\eqref{eq:assumption},
so it applies to every statistic built that way, including those of
REGENIE \citep{mbatchou2021}. We measured it only at $\alpha = 1\%$ per
quintile, and only in the programs we could run; its size at genome-wide
thresholds and in the reference BOLT-LMM and REGENIE programs is not
established here. The structure-aware denominator is principled for
BOLT-LMM-inf. For the mixture and elastic-net statistics its transfer
is a heuristic, and through LDAK-KVIK's scaling rule it left about 7\%
overall inflation under strong simulated structure.

The S2 results restate an old caution in measured terms: a kinship-only
mixed model cannot carry a strong environment on one ancestry axis, and a
principal-component covariate can \citep{price2010,yang2014,sul2018}.
\citet{loh2015} simulated an environment on the top principal component
that explained 1\% of the variance and found the mixed-model statistics
calibrated; S2's 50\% is far beyond that. The S2 results add a second
caution. Comparing LOCO with non-LOCO by $\lgc$ alone
misleads under structure, because the non-LOCO scan's deflation can
cancel residual confounding.

Two simulation designs that look natural produced misleading results.
A confounder placed on the leading eigenvector of the tested SNPs' own
kinship is a function of those SNPs: the SNPs that load on it most carry
the confounder themselves, and only a kinship that contains them absorbs
it. LD blocks cut at fixed intervals of a contiguous segment leak QTL
tags across ``chromosomes'', which inflates false loci and leaks into
LOCO groups. An independent ancestry axis and an LD-optimal split
address both. Unlinked blocks, as in S2, remove the leakage entirely.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The real data come from one inbred plant panel
with strong structure and $n = 1{,}307$. The only reference program we
ran is LDAK-KVIK; BOLT-LMM and REGENIE binaries were not available on
this platform. Our two-step statistics are re-implementations, with
documented departures. The simulations are small (three seeds per
scenario, six replicates for calibration), the infinitesimal background
makes some false loci real, and the environment in S2 explains 50\% of
the variance, which is a stress test rather than a typical cohort.

\paragraph{What would change these conclusions.} Three findings would.
First, a flat loading gradient for the reference BOLT-LMM and REGENIE
programs on the same RegMap phenotypes would confine the problem to
the implementations studied here. Second, a flat gradient in a human
multi-ancestry cohort would limit it to strongly structured samples.
Third, a calibrated exact LOCO scan without principal components when
the environment explains a few percent of the variance would mark S2 as
a stress test only.
